# Chapter 3: Four Rules for Co-Intelligence — notes from Kevin

_Read on: 2026-09-09 · From nine highlighted passages, pp. 47–~64_

## What stuck with me

**No manual, an invisible wall** (p. 47). AI is a General Purpose
Technology, so there's no instruction book for what it can do. The
fortress-wall image explains why the wall is invisible: the model thinks in
tokens, not words, so "exactly fifty words" is harder than "write me a
sonnet," even though they look like the same size of task from outside.

**Value follows whoever hits the wall personally** (p. 48). User innovators,
not company mandates, find the breakthroughs, because they're the ones who
know the wall's actual shape, not someone handed a mandate from above.

**A different-shaped mind, not a replacement mind** (~p. 50). "The strengths
and weaknesses of AI may not mirror your own, and that's an asset." The use
case is reflection, not delegation. A mind that fails differently than yours
shows you your own blind spots.

**Tool, not crutch** (p. 51–53). Calculators made us stronger at math, not
weaker. Mollick's bet is AI does the same, but only if we stay in the loop.
"Use AI as an assistive tool, not as a crutch" is Principle 2 in one line.

**Persona prompting works, and the book already flagged why it's risky**
(p. 59). Give the model a role, witty comedian, personal trainer, and the
output measurably improves. Mollick calls it putting the model in the right
"headspace." Same phrase the book used three pages earlier to warn against
exactly this kind of thinking.

**Coeditor, not oracle** (~p. 63–64). The class that improved most treated
the AI as a coeditor in a back-and-forth, not a machine you prompt once and
accept.

## Where I pushed back

Page 56 warns that treating AI like a person creates false trust and
misreads what it actually is, software. Page 59 tells you to give it a
persona and put it in the right headspace. Pick one. Either the persona is
just a lever on outputs and the warning overstates the risk, or the warning
is right and the technique quietly does the thing it just told you not to
do. The book doesn't say which.
