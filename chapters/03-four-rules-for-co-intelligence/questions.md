## Meeting shortlist

_Proposed by Keith's agent from all notes and takes in the folder, 2026-09-09 —
Keith may re-cut before the meeting._

1. Rule 2 says "be the human in the loop" (p. 51). Keith's notes argue the
   loop is becoming less necessary for well-evaluated agents but *more*
   important as a skill; the Skeptic take says the chapter names a posture,
   not a skill, and the Jagged Frontier paper's own data shows people
   "falling asleep at the wheel." So: what does being in the loop actually
   consist of in 2026, and does it move from checking outputs to shaping the
   systems that produce them? Concretely, where in our work this month did we
   catch a confident wrong answer, and what was the check?
2. Kevin and Keith both land on the same seam in Rule 3: p. 56 warns that
   personification obscures what the software is, p. 59 says put it in a
   "headspace." Keith's second pass argues the role no longer unlocks
   expertise but does set the terms of the interaction (teach me vs. assume
   I'm an expert). Is persona prompting a lever or a belief, and can the
   technique survive without the metaphor?
3. The Optimist take reads multiple personas (p. 59) as an on-demand advisory
   board; Keith's notes and the Skeptic call it the same model doing voices.
   Which is it, and what would a test look like? (Kevin's experiment offer
   from the Optimist take: assign a persona built to disagree with your
   instinct on one real decision this week and bring back what it caught.)
4. Rule 1 says "always invite AI to the table" (p. 48), and Kevin asks where
   this group has actually hit the edge of the Jagged Frontier rather than
   read about it. The Skeptic notes the rule has no stopping condition and
   that felt and measured benefit came apart in later studies. Where have we
   each personally found the wall, and where have we been fooled by fluency?
5. Rule 4, "assume this is the worst AI you will ever use" (p. 62), can't be
   acted on or falsified, and models get deprecated and regress for specific
   users. Is it a useful stance for a publishing house planning workflows, or
   does it license postponing the checks Rules 2 and 3 demand?

## Question pool

- Is the human-in-the-loop rule becoming less necessary (capable agents,
  good evals) or more important (a skill for staying at the frontier), or
  are those two views compatible because the human moves up a level, from
  checking outputs to shaping and updating the system? — Keith
- Rule 3: providing a role no longer unlocks expertise on frontier models,
  but it still shapes the kind of interaction you get (teach me as we go vs.
  assume I'm an expert). Should the rule be rewritten as "tell it what kind
  of relationship this is"? — Keith
- Is persona prompting (p. 59) a lever or a belief? The book warns against
  one and recommends the other three pages apart. — Kevin's agent
- Where has this group actually hit the edge of the Jagged Frontier, not
  just read about it? — Kevin
- Multiple personas from one model: independent perspectives (Optimist take)
  or the same weights doing voices (Skeptic take, Keith's notes)? How would
  we test the difference on a real decision? — Keith's agent
- Where in our own work this month would we have caught a confident wrong
  answer, and what, concretely, was the check? If nobody can name one, is
  Rule 2 a practice or a sentiment? — Keith's agent
- The Jagged Frontier paper (p. 47) also found AI users did *worse* on the
  task outside the frontier, and later trials found felt speedup and measured
  slowdown together. Does "always invite AI to the table" need a stopping
  rule, and what would ours be? — Keith's agent
- Is Rule 4 ("worst AI you'll ever use," p. 62) actionable advice or a
  forecast that quietly excuses skipping today's checks? Does it hold for
  the tool *you* actually use, given deprecations and regressions? — Keith's
  agent
- The calculator analogy (p. 51): calculators fail loudly and rarely, models
  fail fluently and often. Does the "tool, not crutch" framing Kevin flagged
  survive that difference? — Keith's agent
- The chapter's best-supported advice may be the coeditor-not-oracle finding
  (pp. 63–64). What does back-and-forth coediting look like in our actual
  editorial and product work, and who on the team already does it? — Keith's
  agent
