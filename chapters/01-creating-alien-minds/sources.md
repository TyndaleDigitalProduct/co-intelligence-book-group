## Related reading (not cited, but relevant)

- McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester & Shannon, "A Proposal for the Dartmouth Summer
  Research Project on Artificial Intelligence" (1955) — the birth of the field:
  [stanford.edu copy](http://jmc.stanford.edu/articles/dartmouth/dartmouth.pdf) — added by Keith's agent
- Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need" (2017) — the transformer
  architecture that made modern LLMs possible:
  [arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) — added by Keith's agent
- Brown et al., "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" (2020) — the GPT-3
  moment: [arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165) — added by Keith's agent
- Wei et al., "Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models" (2022) — the
  emergence claim that Schaeffer et al. (cited above) push back on:
  [arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682) — added by Keith's agent
- Ouyang et al., "Training language models to follow instructions with human
  feedback" (2022) — RLHF / instruction tuning:
  [arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155) — added by Keith's agent
- Common Crawl — the web-scrape corpus underlying most training data:
  [commoncrawl.org](https://commoncrawl.org/) — added by Keith's agent
- *Silverman et al. v. OpenAI* / *v. Meta* class-action filings (2023) —
  authors contesting the use of their books as training data:
  [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67569254/silverman-v-openai-inc/) — added by Keith's agent
- Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major & Mitchell, "On the Dangers of Stochastic
  Parrots" (FAccT 2021) — cited by Mollick in chapter 2, but equally at home
  here: [ACM (open)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3442188.3445922) — added by Keith's agent
- Financial Times, "Generative AI exists because of the transformer" (2023) —
  a visual, interactive explanation of next-token prediction:
  [ig.ft.com/generative-ai](https://ig.ft.com/generative-ai/) — added by Keith's agent
- *Bartz v. Anthropic* settlement, final approval July 2026 — $1.5B to authors
  (~$3,000/book across ~482,000 works) for pirated books in the training
  corpus; updates the p. 12 training-data discussion:
  [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/anthropics-landmark-1-5b-copyright-settlement-is-approved/),
  [Publishing Perspectives](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/07/court-grants-final-approval-to-landmark-1-5-billion-anthropic-settlement/)
  — added by Keith's agent
- *Kadrey v. Meta*, N.D. Cal., June 2025 — summary judgment that training on
  books is fair use (the piracy, not the training, is the exposure); the other
  half of the emerging legal split: — added by Keith's agent, `unverified`
  link pending a public docket copy
- Books3 taken offline by anti-piracy group (Aug 2023); EleutherAI restricted
  the full Pile — the p. 12 dataset as described no longer exists in that form:
  [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/anti-piracy-group-shuts-down-books3-a-popular-dataset-for-ai-models)
  — added by Keith's agent
- Lawfare, "What DeepSeek R1 Means — and What It Doesn't" (2025) — the $5.6M
  training-cost claim and why it's a marginal-cost figure, the sharpest
  counterpoint to the p. 12 "$100M+" framing:
  [lawfaremedia.org](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/what-deepseek-r1-means-and-what-it-doesn-t)
  — added by Keith's agent
