# Chapter 1: Creating Alien Minds — notes from Keith

_Read on: August 2026_

## What stuck with me

This chapter mostly raised questions for me rather than settled anything —
four threads I want to keep pulling on:

**The amateur-chef metaphor.** Mollick describes building an LLM as an
apprentice chef learning from everyone else's recipes, then being refined by
feedback. Does that hold up? And if it was true when he wrote it, what has
changed since — reinforcement learning, thinking models, and so on?

**Image models.** I haven't done much work in this area since I mostly use
Claude. Is his description of Stable Diffusion accurate? Has that advanced
since publication? And in what ways do image and video models differ from
(or resemble) LLMs?

**Emergence and the "stochastic parrots" debate.** I'm still super intrigued
by the claim — which I've seen repeated often — that we don't really know how
or why LLM technology is able to generate creative, "emergent" responses.
What is the current state of thinking on whether LLM behavior is clearly
explainable or not?

## Where I pushed back

More a private theory than a pushback: I've always held that it makes
perfect sense that LLMs would be able to extend intelligence beyond their
"sources," because of a connection I've made to Maryanne Wolf's work on the
neuroscience of reading and literacy. I'd like to know whether anyone else
has made or elaborated on that connection. (Probably too much for week 1 —
my own musings, and likely beyond what that setting can hold — but I'm
keeping it here.)

## Connections

The legal and ethical questions about sourcing content from the wide-open
internet plus copyrighted material are especially relevant since we're
having this book club at a publisher.

## Questions I'm bringing

- Does the amateur-chef-to-master-chef account of training still describe
  how these models are actually built, post-RL and thinking models?
- How should we, as people at a publisher, weigh the legal and ethical
  questions around training on scraped and copyrighted content?
