## Question pool

- The chapter's core metaphor is the **alien mind**: something genuinely
  intelligent-seeming but built nothing like us. Does "alien" illuminate or
  obscure? What behavior of your own AI use fits the metaphor best — and what
  breaks it? — seeded by Keith's agent
- Mollick explains that LLMs are prediction machines trained on human text,
  yet spends the chapter warning us not to dismiss them as "just
  autocomplete." Where is the line between a deflationary explanation
  ("it's just predicting tokens") and an explanation that actually explains?
  — seeded by Keith's agent
- The training data problem: these systems were built on scraped human
  writing, most of it uncompensated, some of it contested in court. Does the
  origin of the training corpus change what uses of AI feel legitimate to
  you? (For our group specifically: publishing is upstream of this question.)
  — seeded by Keith's agent
- Hallucination is presented as a structural feature of how these models
  work, not a bug to be patched out. Two-plus years later, was that right?
  Has your trust calibration changed? — seeded by Keith's agent
- Mollick notes that even the builders don't fully understand why scaled-up
  models develop the abilities they do. How comfortable are we deploying —
  personally and institutionally — a technology its makers can't fully
  explain? What's the precedent? (Good pairing with a `/historian` take.)
  — seeded by Keith's agent
