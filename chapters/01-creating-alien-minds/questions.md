## Meeting shortlist

_(picked by Keith, facilitating, for the 2026-08-12 kickoff meeting on
chapters 0–1)_

1. The chapter explains these systems through two metaphors — the **alien
   mind** and the **amateur chef** apprenticing on everyone else's recipes.
   Which one matches your actual experience of using AI, where do they break,
   and does the chef story still describe how models are built now
   (reinforcement learning, thinking models)?
2. Mollick repeats the claim that nobody fully knows why LLMs produce
   creative, "emergent" responses — the "stochastic parrots" debate. Two-plus
   years on: do we know more now, and how comfortable are we using a
   technology whose makers can't fully explain it?
3. The training corpus is scraped human writing, much of it uncompensated and
   contested in court. As people at a publisher — upstream of this question —
   where do our obligations to authors and our practical use of these tools
   actually sit?

## Question pool

- The chapter's core metaphor is the **alien mind**: something genuinely
  intelligent-seeming but built nothing like us. Does "alien" illuminate or
  obscure? What behavior of your own AI use fits the metaphor best — and what
  breaks it? — seeded by Keith's agent
- Mollick explains that LLMs are prediction machines trained on human text,
  yet spends the chapter warning us not to dismiss them as "just
  autocomplete." Where is the line between a deflationary explanation
  ("it's just predicting tokens") and an explanation that actually explains?
  — seeded by Keith's agent
- The training data problem: these systems were built on scraped human
  writing, most of it uncompensated, some of it contested in court. Does the
  origin of the training corpus change what uses of AI feel legitimate to
  you? (For our group specifically: publishing is upstream of this question.)
  — seeded by Keith's agent
- Hallucination is presented as a structural feature of how these models
  work, not a bug to be patched out. Two-plus years later, was that right?
  Has your trust calibration changed? — seeded by Keith's agent
- Mollick notes that even the builders don't fully understand why scaled-up
  models develop the abilities they do. How comfortable are we deploying —
  personally and institutionally — a technology its makers can't fully
  explain? What's the precedent? (Good pairing with a `/historian` take.)
  — seeded by Keith's agent
- Does the amateur-chef-to-master-chef account of training still describe how
  these models are actually built — and if it was true when Mollick wrote it,
  what has changed since (reinforcement learning, thinking models, etc.)?
  — Keith
- Mollick's tour includes image models like Stable Diffusion. For those of us
  who live mostly in text models: is his description accurate, how has that
  world advanced since publication, and where do image and video models
  genuinely differ from LLMs versus rhyme with them? — Keith's agent, from
  Keith's notes
- The "stochastic parrots" debate: Mollick repeats the claim that we don't
  really know how or why LLMs generate creative, "emergent" responses. What
  is the current state of thinking on whether LLM behavior is explainable —
  and has interpretability research since 2024 moved the line? — Keith
- How should people at a publisher weigh the legal and ethical questions
  around models trained on scraped and copyrighted content — where do our
  obligations to authors and our practical use of these tools actually sit?
  — Keith
