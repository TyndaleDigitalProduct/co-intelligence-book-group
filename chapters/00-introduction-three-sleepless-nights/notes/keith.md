# Introduction: Three Sleepless Nights — notes from Keith

_Read on: August 2026_

## What stuck with me

My first big "sleepless night" phase wasn't from my early interactions with
LLMs. Those were exciting, and early like Mollick's, but they didn't keep me
up thinking about possibilities. It came from the Opus 4.5 release — the
first time I felt like I could really unleash my product thinking into useful
code with low friction. I spent a few days listening to podcasts and reading
docs, then went from an idea to a shipped TestFlight version of an iOS app in
36 hours, without ever having written a line of Swift in my life. (The app:
personal ratings and reviews of local restaurants, designed to help me and my
wife decide where to go for dinner quickly.)

## Where I pushed back

I'm still not as freaked out as Mollick was by the negotiation-teacher
prompt. To me, getting 80% of the way to a tool like the one he describes is
really more like 10% of the work it takes to build something valuable at his
level of expertise in that topic. So I don't see much danger of domain
expertise being devalued by LLMs in their current form.

The historical perspective on the productivity impact of transformative
technologies was interesting, but I was sometimes left thinking the analysis
was the culprit in not finding productivity leaps. No matter what the
research says, the Internet has had a transformative effect on people, work,
and value creation.
