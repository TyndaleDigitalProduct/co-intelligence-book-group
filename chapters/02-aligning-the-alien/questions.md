1. Mollick refuses to adjudicate between the apocalypse and techno-utopia
   poles (p. 31), and the Skeptic take argues that "not-knowing licenses
   caution as readily as enthusiasm." Keith's notes suggest a biblical view
   lets us dismiss the apocalyptic pole but demands equal nuance toward the
   utopian one. So: what does honest uncertainty actually *oblige* us to do —
   and does Christian eschatology change what "proceeding under uncertainty"
   looks like? (Warm-up: is *Mrs. Davis* a better alignment story than the
   paperclip maximizer?)
2. Both members' notes conclude the courts settled the wrong question: Keith
   ("is the legal line the ethical line?" — the settlement priced acquisition,
   not the unpaid labor), Kevin ("legality is a venue, not a standard… most
   companies don't ask," p. 33). For a publishing house: what would *asking*
   look like in practice — and is anything of ours reserved in a
   machine-readable way?
3. The chapter's working answer to unsolved alignment is the human in the
   loop (p. 43), but the Skeptic take shows vigilance decays as systems
   improve, and Kevin's notes admit the same from the desk: "my shortcut is
   the institution's defect." What would we have to build — at the house, not
   in the abstract — for our loop to be real rather than nominal?
4. Keith's notes argue "unbiased AI" is a view-from-nowhere Christians
   shouldn't believe in; Kevin's notes show the worst bias arrives as a
   *silent competence failure* (the pronoun test, p. 36) that tuning can't
   catch. If neutrality is a worldview and the dangerous bias is invisible,
   what does faithful institutional use look like — confessional framing,
   editorial judgment, both?
